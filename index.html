Predicting Loan Approval (Exploratory Modeling)

Results below are the exact values supplied for a separate modeling subset. They have not been reproduced from the current local extract because the populations do not match.

Modeling subset: 10,993 applications with clear outcomes (9,973 approved, 1,020 cancelled; 90.7% approved). Target: approved (1) vs cancelled (0). Stratified 80/20 train/test split with random_state=42.

The majority baseline has the highest raw accuracy because approved cases dominate the subset, so accuracy alone is misleading. Macro-F1 weights both classes equally; models are compared by macro-F1 and ROC-AUC. Extra Trees ranked highest on both supplied measures (0.558 and 0.674). ROC-AUC around 0.67 suggests limited separation from application-time fields. Workflow or customer factors could contribute to cancellations, but that is a hypothesis to test, not a conclusion.

Features and evaluation

Numeric application-time features: loan amount, monthly, rebate, gross loan, total interest, old account, total deductions, net amount, term, interest rate, pension amount, age at application (clipped to 18–100), number of months, and dependents. Categorical features: loan type, branch, gender, civil status, pension code, purpose, employer, bank code, withdraw-all flag, and residence certificate. Group-level names and values are not displayed.

Numeric preprocessing: median imputation and standard scaling. Categorical preprocessing: most-frequent imputation and one-hot encoding. Class weighting was used for imbalance. Decision and workflow fields, approval status, and remarks were excluded to avoid target leakage.

Compared models: majority baseline, Logistic Regression, Random Forest, Extra Trees, and XGBoost. Limitations: one split, no tuning or cross-validation, no probability calibration, and approximately 1,020 minority-class examples. Do not use these results to make lending decisions.
